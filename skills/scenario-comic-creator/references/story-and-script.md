# Story and script

Write the whole script before any page art. Style tests may use two sample pages from it. Finding
the specific story and voice comes first: [art-direction.md](art-direction.md).

## From premise to pages

1. **Logline**: who wants what, and what stands in the way.
2. **Beat sheet**: setup, inciting event, escalation, turn, climax, landing. One beat is one page or
   one spread.
3. **Page budget**: assign beats to pages. Cut beats, not panels, when the count is too high.
4. **Page scripts**: one file per page, written in the format below.

A six-page story (a short gift comic):

| Page | Beat                                  |
| ---- | ------------------------------------- |
| 1    | Ordinary world, hero and want         |
| 2    | The inciting event                    |
| 3    | Crossing into the new place           |
| 4    | Escalation: the obstacle bites        |
| 5    | Turn and climax                       |
| 6    | Landing: the changed world, last line |

A 24-page story stretches each beat over about four pages and adds a midpoint reversal. A book adds
chapters, each with its own landing.

## Page craft

- One idea per page. End on a beat: a reveal, a question or a laugh.
- The turn of the page is a reveal. Put the surprise on the first panel of a left-hand page when the
  edition prints as a book.
- Open a sequence on a wide panel that fixes the geography. Climb to closer shots as tension rises.
  Add an insert for a key object and a reaction for the listener.
- Hold one side of the axis in a scene so screen direction never flips without a reason.
- In showpiece mode, add silent pages. A full-page illustration with no balloons gives rhythm. Plan
  at least two per chapter in a long book. In clear-first mode a silent page carries its beat in a
  caption.
- Four to six panels per page is the reliable range. Past seven, panels shrink and lettering
  suffers.
- Assign emotion per panel from what just happened. A reference smile is not an instruction.

## Script check

Run this on the finished script, before any art:

- Every printable word is in the script file, spelled as it will print.
- Cover the art and read only the captions and balloons, in order. A newcomer can say what happens,
  why it matters and what to do. If not, rewrite the words before any page runs.
- Every page names one dominant panel.
- Showpiece mode adds: a panel with no words on every page, no two consecutive pages sharing a grid
  or a camera setup, and a silent page in a book of six pages or more.
- Clear-first mode adds: every page has one learning beat, every caption has a job written beside
  it, captions sit in fewer than half of a page's panels, and the last page holds the takeaways
  ([clear-first.md](clear-first.md)).

## Dialogue

- Short balloons read faster and letter more reliably. Aim for about 15 words per balloon, two or
  three balloons per panel, authoring-time defaults. Clear-first mode keeps to two.
- Write full sentences that say the thing: the action, the object and the reason. A clipped line
  that only makes sense with the art fails a reader who is not a comic fan.
- One speaker per balloon. Say who speaks and in which order they are read.
- Narration goes in caption rectangles, kept apart from speech. A caption sets the place, the time
  and the stake. Balloons carry speech only.
- Sound effects are lettering too. Write each one into the script or leave it out.
- Printable words live in the script file and are pasted unchanged into every redraw.

## Page script format

```
PAGE 7   place: kitchen   time: night   layout: 3 panels, one dominant (P1)
Light: oil lamp at the far end of the table, warm; window left, flat cold gray
In: Ines in a nightgown, empty hands; grandfather seated, hands flat on a letter
Must be visible: the letter, the lamp, Ines's shadow on the floor

P1  wide, low camera from the doorway. Ines small in the foreground, back to
    us, her shadow reaching the table. Grandfather at the lamp.
P2  insert on the letter under his hands, thumbs pressing the corners.
    Caption: "Eleven stamps, none of them ours."
P3  close on Ines, lamplight on one cheek. She wants to ask and is afraid
    of the answer, lips pressed.
    Ines: "Who is it from?"
Out: Ines in a nightgown, one hand on the table edge; grandfather seated,
     letter still under his hands
```

- Each panel names the camera, the action phase, who is present, gaze, expression and object state.
- **In** and **Out** list costume, held objects and injuries. A page's In equals the previous page's
  Out: an object thrown on page 3 is not in hand on page 4.
- Production notes (references, attempts, checks) stay in a separate file from the printable words.

## Adapting a source text

- Build a coverage table: source section, pages, or "cut" with the reason. Review it with the user
  before page 1.
- Take dialogue from the text. Never invent lines. Move narration into captions.
- Plan each chapter's page budget up front. Restart page numbers per chapter when the book does.
- Record every adaptation (a merged scene, a moved line) so a reviewer can tell a choice from an
  error.
- Check callbacks against the pages already adapted. Quotations do not automatically alternate
  speakers.

## Personalized stories

- Use only facts the user supplied about real people: names, pets, places, shared jokes.
- Add a dedication page and a last page that lands on the person.
- Ask for one detail per person that only they would recognize. It makes the comic theirs.
