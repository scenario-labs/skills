# Clear-first comics

Use this mode when the readers did not choose a comic: employees, customers, students, a board. They
read once, in a few minutes, to learn or decide something. A comic they cannot follow has failed,
whatever the art. Readability beats art on every page. The art keeps a hand, inside the limits
below.

Typical jobs: training, onboarding, security and safety awareness, compliance, sales aids, internal
communication, explainers for a complex topic.

## Message first

The brief names the reader, what they should know or do afterwards, and at most three takeaways. Ask
for them at intake. Take them from the client's words and invent no procedure.

- Every page carries one learning beat, stated once in plain words: by a character when it sounds
  natural, otherwise in a caption.
- The last page ends on a takeaway rectangle: the rules, in the client's words.
- A joke or an image may carry feeling, never the information.

## The lettering kit

Three containers, each with one job. Write each into the prompt as its own object, so the model
never picks the container.

| Container         | Shape and place                                       | Holds                                 |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------- |
| Speech balloon    | Round, white, tail to the speaker                     | Spoken words, one speaker per balloon |
| Caption rectangle | Square corners, cream fill, top-left of the panel     | Narration: where, when, what and why  |
| Label rectangle   | Small square box beside a figure, or a card on screen | A name and role, a message, a button  |

- A scene opens with a caption that says the place and the time, unless the picture already shows
  both. How many more: see the next section.
- Name each character in a label at first appearance, with the role the reader needs ("Mara, growth
  lead").
- Show a chat message, an alert or a button as a large readable card at page size. Small screen text
  is never legible.
- Reading order is caption, then balloons, top left to bottom right.
- Aim for 40 to 70 printed words per page, captions included. Under 25, the page explains nothing.
  Over 90, the lettering shrinks. These are authoring-time defaults.

## How many rectangles

Captions are a budget you assess page by page, never a slot in every panel. With none, a reader who
is not a comic fan gets lost. With too many, the page reads like slides with pictures.

- Most panels carry no caption. A typical page has one or two. One caption per panel is almost never
  right.
- A caption earns its place only with a job the picture and the balloons cannot do:
  - a new place, or a jump in time;
  - a term the reader does not know;
  - the page's learning beat, when no character can say it naturally.
- Cut a caption that repeats what the picture shows or a balloon says. Prefer a character saying it.
- Count every rectangle together: captions, labels and screen cards. A panel that already holds a
  screen card or a name label needs no caption.
- Assess it in the script. For each page, list each caption with its job, and cut any caption with
  none. Captions in more than half of a page's panels are too many: keep the one or two that carry
  the most.

## Plain dialogue

- Write full sentences. The characters name the action, the object and the reason once.
- Define a technical term at first use, in one clause inside a caption.
- Use roles and plain nouns. A codename or an in-joke goes only where a caption explains it.
- Keep each balloon under about 15 words and each panel at two balloons or fewer.
- Banter sits beside facts, never in place of them.
- One name per thing. A character, a bot or a tool keeps the same name in captions, cards and
  balloons.
- A reply needs its question on the page. A "No." that answers nothing reads as a mistake.
- Earn every takeaway: an event in the story causes it. A takeaway the plot never touches reads as a
  loose analogy.

Before and after, the same panel:

```
Cryptic    MARA: "Tomorrow. The launch first."
           (a laptop pop-up, no caption: a reader cannot tell what is postponed, or why it matters)

Clear      Caption: "Thursday, 1:10 a.m. A security update has waited on Mara's laptop for six days."
           MARA: "I'll restart after the launch. It can wait one more night."
```

One caption sets the time and the stake, and Mara's line says the choice. The lesson lands later on
the page, in one line from the colleague who has to clean up, not in a second caption.

## Cover-the-art test

Run it on the script, before any art. Read only the captions and the balloons, in order. A newcomer
can say what happens, why it matters and what to do. If not, fix the words. A page cannot repair a
script.

## Art limits that protect reading

- **Light ground.** Paper or a light flat is the largest area on every page. Dark inks are shapes on
  that ground. A dark panel is a beat, never a whole page, and no page is more than a third near
  black.
- **Lettering on light.** Balloons and captions sit on light ground with dark text. Never place text
  on a dark mass.
- **The lesson is the clearest object.** The thing the lesson is about (the key, the form, the
  button) is the largest, cleanest shape in its panel and takes the spot color if there is one.
- **Plain geography.** The first panel of each scene shows the place so a reader knows where they
  are.
- **Three to five panels,** one clear reading order, no tiny inserts for key information.
- **Distinct figures.** Each character differs by silhouette and one costume color, readable at
  thumbnail size.
- **Directions.** Pick from the clear family in [style-library.md](style-library.md). Dense or dark
  directions bury lettering.
- Clarity beats variety: two pages may share a grid, and a silent page needs a caption that carries
  its beat.

## The cold read

This gate replaces per-page taste rounds. A reviewer who has seen neither the script nor the brief
gets the finished pages in order and writes:

1. One sentence per page: what happens.
2. The point of the comic.
3. Who each character is.
4. Every place they guessed: a balloon they could not read, a missing step, a joke they missed.
5. Every caption that told them what they could already see, or that they skipped.

Compare it with the beats and takeaways in the brief card. A page fails when the sentence misses its
beat or the reviewer guessed. Fix with words first: rewrite or add a caption through a text repair
([page-production.md](page-production.md)). Redraw only when the art hides the beat. After two
failed cold reads, show the user the sentences and ask. When someone from the audience is available,
a second cold read from that person is worth more than any score.

## Taste in this mode

- Run the taste review twice: on the style anchor, and on the finished book's contact sheet.
- Render the stock twin for page 1 only.
- A Competent page ships when it reads. A Generic page is redrawn once.
