# Personalized and branded comics

Two common short-lane jobs: a gift comic that stars a real person, and a comic that carries a
company's message. The Quick lane covers both. This file adds what each needs on top.

## Personalized gift comic

- Intake adds: the recipient's name, age band, a photo, pets and places, two shared jokes, the
  occasion, a dedication.
- Use only facts the buyer supplied. Never invent biography, health, family or events for a real
  person. Unattended, with few details, use what the brief gives and invent no shared jokes.
- The hero sheet comes from the photo, approved by the buyer before any page. The sheet carries the
  likeness. Pages use the sheet.
- Offer the buyer creative directions for title and plot, not only a style. Three loglines with
  titles is enough.
- Keep the story to one clear arc and a last page that lands on the person.
- Build the story on one true detail from the facts supplied, and take an angle on it that a
  template would not. See [art-direction.md](art-direction.md).
- Check the printed name on every page. A name is the one word a reader will not forgive.
- A child's photo: the buyer is the parent or guardian.

## Branded or training comic

Use cases: onboarding, safety and compliance, brand history, sales aids, sustainability and CSR,
recruiting, explainers for a complex topic. Typical length is 8 to 48 pages.

The readers are staff or customers who must understand a message, not comic fans. Work in
clear-first mode ([clear-first.md](clear-first.md)): at most three takeaways in the client's words,
plain dialogue, a few caption rectangles where the balloons need them, light pages, and a cold read
before delivery. The client reads the script's captions and balloons alone before any art is drawn.

### Approve in this order

1. **Brief**: audience, one message, must-say, must-avoid, tone, language, length, delivery date.
2. **Storyboard**: the cast sheets, the page-by-page script and two finished sample pages. The
   client approves this.
3. **Pages**: produced only after approval.

A change at the storyboard costs a line in the script. The same change after the pages costs pages.

### Brand fidelity

- A brand's palette and logo rules are constraints on the look bible, not the look itself. The comic
  still needs its own light, camera and voice.
- A logo, a product, a packaging label or a uniform is never drawn freehand. Use the product photo
  as a reference, check the label at crop, and composite exact brand assets over the art
  (`scenario-product-shots` for products, `scenario-text-overlay` for exact text).
- Write a one-page brand sheet: palette in hex, tone of voice, forbidden topics, logo rules.
- Employees, founders or customers as characters need their written consent. A mascot avoids the
  question.

### Facts and compliance

- Every number, claim and procedure on a page comes from the client's brief. Never invent
  statistics.
- Safety and compliance comics get a subject-matter review of the finished pages before release.
- Keep the lettering editable (overlay mode) when the content will be updated.

### Languages

One story, several editions: overlay mode, one lettering pass per language, a fluent reader per
language. See [assembly-and-delivery.md](assembly-and-delivery.md).

### Deliver

- Reading PDF, print file at the client printer's spec, and a web version if it will be posted.
- Keep the script, the sheets and the lettering source so the client can update pages later.
