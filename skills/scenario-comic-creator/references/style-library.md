# Style library

Paste-ready directions, each tested on the same page: a quiet night scene, a lamp as the only light,
three panels. Take one as a starting point and adapt the hard rules to the story. Keep the FORBIDDEN
list. Pick the quadrant first (see [art-direction.md](art-direction.md)).

Clear-first mode picks from the light, open directions: Hergé and Chaland, Blake and Sempé, Harper,
Bass and Ware, and Blain and Sfar. Keep paper or a light flat as the largest area, and use the dark
color for shapes, never for the ground. Mignola, Pratt and Muñoz, and Tardi swallow lettering in
black or dense hatching, so they suit showpiece mode.

Every paragraph goes under an "ART DIRECTION" heading in the page prompt, after the page layout, the
scene and the panels. The page prompt opens with one line: "The drawing style decides everything.
Commit to it with the confidence, limits and imperfections of a real artist's hand, never a generic
polished digital illustration."

## Black and white, minimal

**Mignola, flat black and two inks.** Strong for night, dread and quiet tension.

```
In the manner of Mike Mignola. Heavy angular brush shapes, simplified blocky forms, and enormous
areas of solid flat black shadow that swallow half of every panel. Palette: solid black, deep teal,
rust red and a touch of dirty cream where the lamp lights things. Faces reduced to a few hard
shapes. Rough, confident brush edges. No rendering, no gradients, no fine detail.
```

**Pratt and Muñoz, pure brush.** Strong for noir, sea stories and long silences.

```
In the manner of Hugo Pratt and José Muñoz: pure black and white, no gray at all. Large expressive
masses of solid black made with a wet brush, white paper used as light, rough dry-brush edges and
a few ink spatters. Faces and hands drawn with a handful of decisive strokes.
```

## Black and white, detailed

**Urasawa and Otomo, printed manga.** Strong for realistic drama and close faces.

```
In the manner of Naoki Urasawa and Katsuhiro Otomo, printed manga with hard rules. Strictly black
ink line and screentone on white paper. Tone exists only as mechanical printed dot screens in three
visible densities, plus solid black. Every object is outlined in clean fine pen line. FORBIDDEN:
smooth gray gradients, airbrush, photographic tone, any gray that is not a visible dot pattern.
```

## Color, minimal

**Hergé and Chaland, ligne claire.** Strong for adventure, humor and clarity.

```
In the manner of Hergé and Yves Chaland, ligne claire. One uniform, perfectly even black ink line
with no variation in weight, no hatching, no gradients. Flat unmodulated color fills only. Shadows
are flat shapes of one darker tone. Palette limited to deep teal, brick red, ochre yellow and cream
paper. Matte newsprint paper, very slight color misregistration.
```

**Blake and Sempé, nervous pen and three colors.** Strong for children's stories, humor and
tenderness.

```
In the manner of Quentin Blake and Jean-Jacques Sempé, with hard rules. Every line is a quick
nervous dip-pen line that vibrates and overshoots. Watercolor uses only three colors: tomato red,
lemon yellow and ink blue, laid in hard-edged patches that do not match the line. Most of the page
is bare white paper. Figures are long-limbed and comic. FORBIDDEN: big shiny eyes, cute baby
proportions, digital polish, gradients, glow.
```

**Harper, Bass and Ware, geometric and one spot color.** Strong for training, explainers and brand
stories.

```
In the manner of Charley Harper and Saul Bass, with the diagrammatic discipline of Chris Ware: flat
geometric shapes with no outlines, mid-century modernist graphic design, a strict grid, large
areas of flat midnight blue and warm gray, and one safety-orange spot color used only for what the
lesson is about. Characters are simplified geometric forms with dot eyes. Tiny, precise lettering.
```

**Blain and Sfar, fast brush.** Strong for comedy, family and movement.

```
In the manner of Christophe Blain and Joann Sfar, with hard rules. The line is the picture: fast,
thick, wobbly brush-pen contours that overshoot and do not close. Faces and bodies are
caricatured: big noses, tiny eyes, heavy brows, long limbs. Color is flat watercolor shapes in
ochre, burnt orange and blue-gray, misaligned with the line, with large areas of bare paper.
FORBIDDEN: realistic shading, glow, gradients, cinematic lighting, smooth anatomy.
```

**Kirby and Ditko, printed pop.** Strong for adventure, superheroes and big gestures.

```
In the manner of Jack Kirby and Steve Ditko, 1960s American adventure comics on cheap paper, with
hard rules. Thick confident black brush ink with big spotted black shapes. Color is four flat
printing inks only (red, yellow, blue, a dull green) and shading is visible Ben-Day dots. Dramatic
foreshortening, speed lines, jagged energy lines. FORBIDDEN: gradients, painterly lighting,
photographic faces, glow, smooth shading.
```

## Color, detailed

**Moebius, ink under color plates.** Strong for strange worlds, deserts and architecture.

```
In the manner of Moebius (Jean Giraud), with hard rules. Every form is built from precise hairline
black ink only: volume comes exclusively from fine parallel hatching and stippling. Color is a
separate flat layer under the ink, like a printed color plate: unblended tints of pale turquoise,
salmon pink and sand, with large calm areas of one flat tint. One acid-yellow flat shape for the
light. FORBIDDEN: cinematic lighting, glow, gradients, soft shading, painterly texture.
```

**Jacobs and Moebius, theatrical adventure.** Strong for storms, machines and grand scale.

```
In the manner of Edgar P. Jacobs (Blake and Mortimer) and Moebius: a precise, theatrical 1950s
adventure-comic look with confident fine ink contours, carefully hand-painted gradations in the
sky, rain drawn as long ruled diagonal lines, detailed machinery. Palette: bruised violet, sea
green and slate, with one sulphur-yellow beam as the only warm light.
```

**Tardi, dense dip pen.** Strong for war, city life and tired faces. Needs the line pushed hard.

```
In the manner of Jacques Tardi, with hard rules. The picture is scratchy dip-pen line work first:
every surface is dense cross-hatching and thousands of tiny uneven strokes, outlines wobble and
never close. Color is a thin flat wash in exactly three tones: slate gray, mustard and tobacco
brown, unblended. FORBIDDEN: painted rendering, smooth shading, glow, gradients, photographic depth.
```

## Test before you trust

Directions that gave a dark cinematic painting without hard rules: Bilal and Rackham with Bilibin,
in the tests that wrote this library. Add hard rules and a FORBIDDEN list to them and run the stock
twin before use.

Untested families to try in a style round: Frank Miller, Charles Burns, Winsor McCay, Will Eisner,
Osamu Tezuka, Taiyō Matsumoto, Junji Ito, Kentaro Miura, Tsutomu Nihei, Shaun Tan, Tove Jansson, E.
H. Shepard. Write each with the same formula and check it against its stock twin.
