# Transformations: the same story, plain and directed

Each pair uses the same story, model, quality and size. Only the prompt changes. The first page of
each pair is what a plain prompt returns: competent and stock. Study the second prompt as a model
for your own.

## Pair 1: a quiet night scene

**Plain prompt**

```
A three-panel comic page for children. A little girl named Ines stands in the doorway of a kitchen
at night. Her grandfather sits at the table reading a letter by a lamp. Panel 2 caption: "Eleven
stamps, none of them ours." Panel 3: Ines says "Who is it from?" in a speech balloon. Watercolor
children's book illustration style, warm, cozy, cute.
```

**What came back.** A big-eyed child in heart-print pajamas, a smiling grandfather, a fridge, a
fruit bowl, a lamp with an even warm glow. Panels 1 and 3 are the same composition. Nothing in it
belongs to this story.

**Directed prompt, structure.**

- A first line: the drawing style decides everything and must read as a real artist's hand.
- The page: one large panel across the top two thirds, two small panels below, white gutters.
- The scene: one oil lamp at the far end of a long table is the only light. Ines is small in the
  doorway, seen from behind, her shadow reaching the table. The grandfather's hands lie flat on the
  letter.
- Panels with camera and acting: wide from the doorway with a low camera; an insert on his hands
  pressing the letter's corners; a close-up of Ines, lamplight on one cheek, wanting to ask and
  afraid of the answer.
- Lettering: only the two texts, spelled exactly, nothing else written anywhere.
- Art direction: one of the paragraphs in [style-library.md](style-library.md).

**What came back.** With the Mignola paragraph, flat black shadow swallows half of each panel and
the lamp is a rust-and-teal island. With Pratt and Muñoz, pure black and white with wet-brush
masses. With ligne claire, one even line and four flat colors. Each is a different book with the
same story.

**The trap.** The same scene with mood words only (Bilal, or Rackham with Bilibin) came back as one
dark cinematic painting, the second stock. The hard rules and the FORBIDDEN list are what give
identity.

## Pair 2: a corporate safety lesson

**Plain prompt**

```
A three-panel educational comic about workplace safety. A new employee on a warehouse loading dock
puts on a safety harness and clips it to the rail. Caption: "Clip first, then climb." She says:
"Click. Got it." Friendly corporate illustration style, bright colors.
```

**What came back.** Clip-art cartoon in a neon vest, a numbered step box, an invented wall sign
("Safe people, stronger teams"), a footer banner with three icons. The model added text and branding
nobody asked for.

**Directed paragraph**

```
In the manner of Charley Harper and Saul Bass, with the diagrammatic discipline of Chris Ware:
flat geometric shapes with no outlines, mid-century modernist graphic design, a strict grid, large
areas of flat midnight blue and warm gray, and one safety-orange spot color used only for what the
lesson is about (the harness, the line, the clip). Characters are simplified geometric forms with
dot eyes. Tiny, precise sans-serif lettering.
```

**What came back.** An isometric dock in midnight blue, two small figures, and orange only on the
harness, the line and the clip. The lesson is the only thing in color. It reads as a designed
object, and it fits a brand without a logo.

## Pair 3: a child's birthday

**Plain prompt**

```
A three-panel comic page for children, for a girl called Léa who turns 8. Her cat Moustache has
eaten the birthday cake. Caption: "Someone had eaten the cake." Léa says: "Moustache!" Cute
watercolor children's book style, funny, colorful.
```

**What came back.** A shiny-eyed girl, a fluffy cat with cream on its face, balloons and bunting, a
pink sign reading "Happy 8 Birthday". Charming, and the same page a thousand gift books contain.

**Directed prompt, the two ideas that changed it.**

- The camera idea: panel 1 is a cutaway dollhouse view of the whole apartment seen from directly
  above, with a trail of pink paw prints winding through every room to the sofa, where the cat sits
  innocent with his back to us.
- The art direction: the Blake and Sempé paragraph in [style-library.md](style-library.md), with its
  three colors, bare paper and FORBIDDEN list.

**What came back.** A floor plan in nervous pen with three colors, a long-limbed girl pointing, a
cat with a perfect alibi expression. The story engine changed with the style: the page is now a
small mystery.

## Pair 4: an adventure page

**Plain prompt**

```
A three-panel comic page. A pilot lands a small biplane next to a lighthouse during a storm.
Caption: "Fuel for four more minutes." The lighthouse keeper says: "You're late. Soup's cold."
Colorful adventure comic style, dramatic, detailed.
```

**What came back.** A strong cinematic painting with a cockpit view. Good, and the same dramatic
digital painting as any other storm.

**Directed, two ways.** The Jacobs and Moebius paragraph gave a bruised violet and sea-green storm
with a tiny biplane under a huge sulphur beam. The Kirby and Ditko paragraph gave four flat printing
inks, Ben-Day dots, speed lines and a foreshortened red biplane. Both are recognizably someone's
page. The second is unmistakable.

## What to take from the pairs

- The plain page is the stock twin. Render it for your own page 1 and compare
  ([art-direction.md](art-direction.md)).
- Directed prompts carry a camera idea, a light source and acting, and the style paragraph on top.
- Hard rules and a FORBIDDEN list matter more than the names.
